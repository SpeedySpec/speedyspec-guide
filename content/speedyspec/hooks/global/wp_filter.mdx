## Behaviour

`$wp_filter` is a proxy object (`SpeedySpec\WpHooks\FilterGlobal`) over the native hook store. Reading it, indexing it, iterating it, unsetting a hook and setting a new `WP_Hook` behave as in WordPress. <Ev scenario="wp_filter_read_and_index"/> <Ev scenario="wp_filter_iterate"/> <Ev scenario="wp_filter_unset_hook"/> <Ev scenario="wp_filter_set_new_hook_object"/>

## Limits

It is an object, not an array: array functions (`array_keys()`, `array_key_exists()`, `in_array()`, `array_pop()`, …) throw a `TypeError`, and `is_array()` is false. Iterate it with `foreach`, and test a hook with `isset()` or `has_filter()`. <Ev source="ss:compat/hooks/breaks.json#L31-L36" hash="54b93d5ce67537aac4a63b115152329265e083c37e3e600e441dc4f2b8368703"/>

## Porting

- Don't replace `$wp_filter` to clear hooks; remove the callbacks of the hooks you own with `remove_all_filters()`. <Ev break="wp_filter_replace_wholesale"/>
- Don't put a plain array at a hook name; on WordPress that fails at the next `add_filter()` on the hook, on SpeedySpec at once. Set a `WP_Hook`, or use `add_filter()`. <Ev break="wp_filter_set_plain_array"/>
- To register hooks before WordPress loads, leave arrays in `$wp_filter` as WordPress documents; SpeedySpec imports them. Don't build `WP_Hook` objects and replace the global with them. <Ev break="wp_hook_build_preinitialized_hooks"/>
- Replace array functions over `$wp_filter` with `foreach` and `isset()`. <Ev source="ss:compat/hooks/breaks.json#L31-L36" hash="54b93d5ce67537aac4a63b115152329265e083c37e3e600e441dc4f2b8368703"/>
