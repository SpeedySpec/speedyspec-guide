## Behaviour

Served by WordPress's PHP; the Rust rules below are not wired. Phase 4 of the posts and meta port made `post.php`'s reading native, but only `post.php`'s functions: PHP on SpeedySpec still runs WordPress's own `functions.php`, which holds this one. <Ev source="ss:crates/speedyspec-wp-posts-php/src/release.rs#L14-L74" hash="2b3494c571a6a44e50d757bea931bec2c8ee3b909e5e1d940b153db3026b04c2"/>

## The Rust rule

The tortoise and hare is private in Rust, inside `find_hierarchy_loop`. It reads parents in PHP's order (the tortoise, then the hare's two steps), ends at a parent of 0 or a missing post, and answers some member of a loop when the tortoise is lapped. Collecting members, it records each ID at its first position, as PHP's `$return[ $id ] = true` does. <Ev source="ss:crates/speedyspec-wp-posts/src/hierarchy.rs#L36-L72" hash="a1587e7ff9edeb16b611df204523c57a8d1ade5bf4fa93f73765dad9730adbb5"/>

No recording calls it directly; the 4 recorded `wp_find_hierarchy_loop()` calls and the 6 `wp_check_post_hierarchy_for_loops()` calls go through it, with their reads compared in order. <Ev test="speedyspec-wp-posts::entity::hierarchy_matches_wordpress"/>

## Limits

- Posts only, with `wp_get_post_parent_id()` as the callback; see [wp_find_hierarchy_loop](/speedyspec/support/wp_find_hierarchy_loop/).
- `$_return_loop` is a flag in WordPress; Rust collects the members only on the second walk.
- `Uncached` reads every post through `find`, as WordPress does with no object cache; phase 4's reader reads the `posts` cache group first. <Ev source="ss:crates/speedyspec-wp-posts/src/reader.rs#L5-L19" hash="09da15425c05c61578f330c352c0763b5ac7394e30c22dffec7fbfac1012ace4"/>
