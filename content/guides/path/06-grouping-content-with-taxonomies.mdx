---
title: Grouping content with taxonomies
summary: Register a taxonomy for your post type, add terms and assign them, query posts by term, and print terms safely.
---

A **taxonomy** is a way of grouping content, and each group in it is a **term**. WordPress comes with two: categories
and tags. Your plugin can add its own, so the books from [Storing your own content](/guides/path/storing-your-own-content/)
can be grouped by genre. Every taxonomy gets an admin screen for its terms, a box in the editor, archive pages and a
place in queries, all from one registration.

## Categories or tags

The first choice is whether terms nest.

- A **hierarchical** taxonomy works like categories. Terms can have parents (Fiction, then Science fiction under it),
  and people pick from a list of existing terms.
- A **flat** taxonomy works like tags. Terms stand alone, and people type new ones freely.

Choose hierarchical when the groups form a fixed outline you or the site's owner control, and flat when people label
content as they go.

## Adding and assigning terms

<Sym id="function:wp_insert_term" /> adds a term, with a parent for a hierarchy. It returns the new term's IDs, or a
`WP_Error` when the name is empty or already taken at that level. <Sym id="function:wp_set_object_terms" /> sets a
post's terms. By default it replaces them; pass `true` as its fourth argument to add to them instead.

<Example id="guides/grouping-content-with-taxonomies/assign-terms" />

Pass term IDs as integers. `wp_set_object_terms()` reads a string as a term's name or slug, and creates the term if
none matches. A numeric string would quietly create a new term called `12`, so cast the IDs, as the example does.

In plugin code you rarely create terms yourself. People add genres in the admin, and your plugin reads them. Creating
terms is for an import, or for a default term your plugin sets up on activation.

## Querying by term

To find posts by term, give `WP_Query` a `tax_query`: the taxonomy, which field you are matching (`slug`, `name` or
`term_id`), and the terms.

<Example id="guides/grouping-content-with-taxonomies/query-by-term" />

In a hierarchical taxonomy, asking for a parent term includes its children's posts too. Dune is in Science fiction,
yet it appears under Fiction. Set `include_children` to `false` when you want only the posts filed directly under the
term.

## Printing terms safely

<Sym id="function:get_the_terms" /> returns a post's terms, `false` when it has none, or an error when the taxonomy
doesn't exist, so check before looping. <Sym id="function:get_term_link" /> gives each term's archive address.

<Example id="guides/grouping-content-with-taxonomies/list-terms-escaped" />

When the term was saved, WordPress stripped the tags out of its name, but the `&` is still there. Escape every name
with `esc_html()` and every link with `esc_url()` as you print them. Terms are typed by people, and may come from an
import that didn't clean them.

The Taxonomy seam isn't ported to SpeedySpec yet, so this guide makes no promise about how taxonomies behave there.
