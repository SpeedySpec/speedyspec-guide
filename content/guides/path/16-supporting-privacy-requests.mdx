---
title: Supporting privacy requests
summary: Plug your plugin's personal data into WordPress's export and erase tools with an exporter and an eraser, and suggest text for the site's privacy policy.
---

Privacy laws give people the right to a copy of the personal data a site holds about them, and to have it erased.
WordPress has tools for both under Tools, Export Personal Data and Erase Personal Data. A site's owner confirms the
person's email address, then runs the request. WordPress gathers its own data, such as comments and the user's
profile, but it can't know what your plugin stores. If your plugin keeps anything about a person, register an
**exporter** and an **eraser** so those tools include it.

The examples keep a reading list and purchase records for each person, in user meta.

## Exporting a person's data

Register an exporter on the <Sym id="filter:wp_privacy_personal_data_exporters" /> filter, with a name people read
and a callback. WordPress calls the callback with the person's email address and a page number, starting at 1. It
returns the items for that page, and `done` once there are no more:

<Example id="guides/supporting-privacy-requests/register-an-exporter" />

WordPress kept calling until the second page said it was done. Paging keeps each request short, so a person with
thousands of items doesn't make one request time out. Each item has:

- a `group_id` and a `group_label`, which put related items under one heading in the export;
- an `item_id`, unique within the group;
- its `data`, a list of names and values, which the export shows as a table.

Find the person by their email address, and remember that they may have no account. Someone who only left a comment
can ask too, so return no items, with `done`, when you have nothing for them. WordPress puts everything the
exporters return into a zip file the person downloads.

## Erasing a person's data

Register an eraser on the <Sym id="filter:wp_privacy_personal_data_erasers" /> filter. Its callback takes the same
email address and page, removes what it can, and reports back:

<Example id="guides/supporting-privacy-requests/register-an-eraser" />

The eraser reports four things:

- `items_removed`: whether it removed anything;
- `items_retained`: whether it kept anything it was asked to remove;
- `messages`: why it kept it, shown to the site's owner;
- `done`: whether this was the last page.

Erasing doesn't have to mean deleting. Some data must be kept, here purchase records for tax law. Say so in a
message, so the site's owner can tell the person. Other data can be kept once it no longer identifies anyone.
<Sym id="function:wp_privacy_anonymize_data" /> replaces a value with an anonymous one of the same type, which keeps a
record's shape without the person in it:

<Example id="support/wp_privacy_anonymize_data/data" />

Erasers change data that can't be recovered, so WordPress only runs them from the Erase Personal Data screen, for
someone who may erase others' data and delete users: an administrator. Don't add a way to start one from anywhere
else.

## Suggesting privacy policy text

A site's privacy policy should say what each plugin collects. Suggest wording with
<Sym id="function:wp_add_privacy_policy_content" />, on `admin_init`. The site's owner sees it in the policy guide, at
Settings, Privacy, and decides what to copy into their policy:

<Example id="admin/wp_add_privacy_policy_content/example" />

Write it for the people who visit the site, not for the site's owner: what you store, why, for how long, and who
else receives it. Translate it with your text domain, as
[Making your plugin translatable](/guides/path/making-your-plugin-translatable/) explains, because it ends up on the
site in the site's language.

The Privacy tools aren't ported to SpeedySpec yet, so this guide makes no promise about how privacy requests are
handled there.
